What participants receive

Lab data, built around you.

Every participant gets a personalised physiology report after their final visit. This is the kind of data elite coaches use — not estimates from a watch algorithm.

01 — Running Economy
Your oxygen cost at pace

How efficiently you use oxygen at your threshold pace. One of the strongest predictors of distance running performance — and something no wearable can measure accurately. Available to you on request after your final visit.

02 — Lactate Threshold
Your exact tipping point

The precise speed and heart rate where your aerobic system starts struggling. Measured from blood — not calculated from a generic formula.

03 — Aerobic Threshold
Your Zone 2 ceiling, in blood

The exact speed and heart rate at which your aerobic base training sits. You can request individual feedback on your lactate threshold and running economy data after your final visit.

The process

Three visits. One clear picture.

You'll visit our lab three times across two to three weeks. Each session is structured and fully supervised — you'll never show up not knowing what to expect.

01
Visit 1 · Lactate Threshold Test
Establish your aerobic threshold

An incremental treadmill test: you run at progressively faster speeds, each 3 minutes long, while we take a small fingertip blood sample at the end of each stage. This determines your individual LT1 speed — the pace used in Visits 2 and 3, and the anchor for your personal report.

🕐 60–90 min 🤘 Fingertip samples 😘 Gas mask worn
02
Visit 2 · Running Economy Test
Standard warm-up, then 10 minutes at your threshold pace

After a standardised movement warm-up, you'll run for 10 minutes at exactly your LT1 speed from Visit 1. We measure oxygen consumption and breathing patterns throughout. No incremental stages, just a steady submaximal run.

🕐 60–90 min 😘 Gas mask worn
03
Visit 3 · Repeat Economy Test
Same run, different warm-up

An identical 10-minute run at your LT1 speed, with a different warm-up beforehand. Comparing gas analysis and breathing data across Visits 2 and 3 is how we answer the research question — and generate your personalised report.

🕐 60–90 min 📊 Report after this
Who can take part?

You may be eligible if you:

  • run at least three times per week
  • can run continuously for 30 minutes
  • are between 18 and 45 years old
  • are free from injury affecting your running
  • have no known contraindications to vigorous exercise testing
  • can attend three visits at Falmer Campus, University of Brighton

About the study

The science behind it.

This study investigates whether a specific type of breathing-focused warm-up affects your running economy and ventilatory responses during exercise.

Your diaphragm does two jobs simultaneously: it drives breathing and it stabilises your core. The warm-up protocol in this study is designed to train that dual role. We want to know if doing so changes how efficiently you run at threshold intensity.

As a participant, you help answer that question — and after your final visit, you can request individual feedback on your results, including your lactate threshold speed and running economy.

Quick answers
Do I need any prior experience with lab testing?

No. You will be familiarised with the treadmill, breathing measurement equipment, and study procedures during Visit 1. Everything is explained before you start and all sessions are supervised.

Is there any risk involved?

The exercise involved is submaximal treadmill running — not a maximal test. There is a small risk of fatigue or breathlessness. Fingertip blood samples may cause brief discomfort but use sterile, single-use equipment. You may stop at any time. A health screening questionnaire is completed before testing begins.

What happens to my data?

You will be assigned a participant ID code so your name is not attached to your physiological data. All electronic data is stored on University OneDrive only. You may request withdrawal of your data up to 31 August 2026. After that point data will have been anonymised and incorporated into group-level analysis.
